# High blood pressure handout

This is a sample education handout for the demo corpus. It is not a personal care plan.

## What the numbers mean

Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing on artery walls. A reading has two numbers. The top number is systolic pressure, when the heart squeezes. The bottom number is diastolic pressure, when the heart rests between beats.

This handout uses these teaching categories for adults:

- Normal: systolic under 120 and diastolic under 80.
- Elevated: systolic 120 to 129 and diastolic under 80.
- Stage 1 hypertension: systolic 130 to 139 or diastolic 80 to 89.
- Stage 2 hypertension: systolic 140 or higher, or diastolic 90 or higher.

A single high reading does not by itself diagnose hypertension. Diagnosis uses repeated measurements, often including readings taken at home.

## Everyday steps described in this handout

- Aim for less sodium. A common teaching target is under 2,300 mg of sodium a day, about one teaspoon of salt, unless a clinician sets a different limit.
- Be physically active on most days. Brisk walking is the example used here.
- If you drink alcohol, keep it within the limit your clinician sets.
- Do not smoke. Smoking damages blood vessels.
- Take medicines only as prescribed. Do not start, stop, or change a dose from this handout.

## When to get urgent help

Seek emergency care for blood pressure symptoms that include chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness or trouble speaking, a severe headache unlike your usual headaches, or sudden vision change. Those symptoms need in-person evaluation. This handout cannot tell you whether a reading is an emergency for you.
